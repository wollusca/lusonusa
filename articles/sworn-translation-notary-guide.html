1. "Sworn" vs certified translation in Portugal

Many people search for a "sworn translator" (tradutor juramentado), but Portugal has no official sworn-translator profession. A translation becomes valid for Portuguese authorities when it is certified.

Under Decree-Law 237/2001 and the Notarial Code, translations can be certified by notaries, Portuguese consulates, registry officers, lawyers (advogados), solicitors (solicitadores) and recognised chambers of commerce. The translator appears before one of them and declares, under oath or on their honour, that the translation is faithful to the original.

In Indonesia it is different. There, penerjemah tersumpah (sworn translator) is a regulated title. A translation made by an Indonesian sworn translator is not automatically a Portuguese certified translation, so check what the receiving authority in Portugal accepts.

2. Documents that usually need translation

  • Civil records: birth certificates (akta kelahiran), marriage certificates (akta perkawinan / buku nikah), family card (Kartu Keluarga), single-status letters.
  • Police certificate: SKCK issued by the Indonesian National Police (Mabes Polri) for use abroad.
  • Education: diplomas (ijazah) and transcripts, for example for recognition with DGES or university enrolment.
  • Company documents: deeds of establishment, NIB and OSS licences, powers of attorney.

Documents in English are sometimes accepted without translation, but this depends on the authority. AIMA, civil registries and universities each have their own rules.

3. The apostille step

Indonesia joined the Hague Apostille Convention with effect from 4 June 2022, and Portugal is also a party. An Indonesian public document with an apostille from Indonesia's competent authority (the Ministry of Law, through its AHU online service) no longer needs legalisation by the Portuguese Embassy.

  • Some documents must first be legalised by the issuing institution or another ministry before they can be apostilled. check per document
  • The apostille usually goes on the Indonesian original. The translation and certification then follow, in Portugal.

4. What a certified translation looks like

A certified translation package usually contains:

  1. The source document (original or certified copy, apostilled where required).
  2. The translation into European Portuguese.
  3. The certificate (termo de certificação de tradução), signed and stamped by the notary or other certifying professional, bound with the translation.

6. Frequently asked questions

Does Portugal have sworn translators?

No. Portugal has no official "sworn translator" profession. A translation becomes official when it is certified: the translator declares before a notary, lawyer, solicitor, registry officer, recognised chamber of commerce or Portuguese consulate that it is a faithful translation, under Decree-Law 237/2001 and the Notarial Code.

Do Indonesian documents need an apostille to be used in Portugal?

Usually yes. Indonesia joined the Hague Apostille Convention with effect from 4 June 2022, and Portugal is also a party. Indonesian public documents with an apostille from Indonesia's Ministry of Law no longer need embassy legalisation for use in Portugal.

Should I get the apostille before or after the translation?

Normally the apostille goes on the Indonesian original first. The document is then translated into European Portuguese and certified in Portugal. If the translation is made in Indonesia, the receiving Portuguese authority may also require an apostille on the translation. Check with the authority that will receive the document.

1. Terjemahan tersertifikasi di Portugal

Berbeda dengan Indonesia, Portugal tidak mengenal profesi "penerjemah tersumpah". Terjemahan diakui instansi Portugal jika disertifikasi (Decreto-Lei 237/2001). Penerjemah menyatakan kesesuaian terjemahan di hadapan notaris, pengacara (advogado), solicitador, petugas catatan sipil, kamar dagang yang diakui, atau konsulat Portugal.

2. Dokumen yang biasanya perlu diterjemahkan

  • Akta kelahiran, akta perkawinan/buku nikah, Kartu Keluarga.
  • SKCK dari Mabes Polri untuk keperluan luar negeri.
  • Ijazah dan transkrip nilai.
  • Akta pendirian perusahaan, NIB, dan surat kuasa.

3. Apostille

Indonesia resmi menjadi pihak Konvensi Apostille sejak 4 Juni 2022, dan Portugal juga merupakan pihak. Dokumen publik Indonesia yang sudah diapostille oleh Kementerian Hukum (layanan AHU) tidak perlu lagi dilegalisasi Kedutaan Portugal. Umumnya, apostille dibubuhkan pada dokumen asli Indonesia, lalu dokumen diterjemahkan ke bahasa Portugis Eropa dan disertifikasi di hadapan notaris Portugal.
