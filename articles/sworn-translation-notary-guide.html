1. "Sworn" vs certified translation in Portugal

Many people search for a "sworn translator", but Portugal has no official sworn-translator profession. A translation becomes valid for Portuguese authorities when it is a certified translation (tradução certificada).

Who can certify a translation:

  • Notaries: Notarial Code (Decree-Law 207/95), art. 172.
  • Lawyers (advogados), solicitors (solicitadores) and recognised chambers of commerce: Decree-Law 237/2001, art. 5, with the same effect as a notary. Chambers of commerce must be recognised under Decree-Law 244/92.
  • Registry officers (conservadores and oficiais de registo): Decree-Law 76-A/2006, art. 38(1). Certifications by lawyers, solicitors and chambers are valid only once registered online (art. 38(3), Portaria 657-B/2006).
  • The Portuguese consulate in the country where the document was issued (DGES).

The translator appears before the certifier and declares, under oath or on their honour, that the translation is faithful to the original. The certification must cover the content of the translation, not just the signatures.

In Indonesia it is different. There, penerjemah tersumpah (sworn translator) is a regulated title under Permenkum 4/2025. A translation made by an Indonesian sworn translator is not automatically a Portuguese certified translation, so check what the receiving authority in Portugal accepts.

2. Documents that usually need translation

  • Civil records: birth certificates (akta kelahiran), marriage certificates (akta perkawinan / buku nikah), family card (Kartu Keluarga), single-status letters.
  • Police certificate: SKCK issued by the Indonesian National Police (Polri). Under Police Regulation (Perpol) 1/2026, each SKCK covers one purpose and one destination country and is valid for 6 months.
  • Education: diplomas (ijazah) and transcripts, for example for recognition with DGES (Decree-Law 66/2018) or university enrolment.
  • Company documents: deeds of establishment, NIB and OSS licences, powers of attorney.

Documents in English are sometimes accepted without translation, but this depends on the authority (Notarial Code art. 44). AIMA, civil registries and universities each have their own rules.

3. The apostille step

Indonesia joined the Hague Apostille Convention (Presidential Regulation 2/2021) with effect from 4 June 2022, and Portugal is also a party. An Indonesian public document with an apostille from Indonesia's competent authority no longer needs legalisation by the Portuguese Embassy. The authority is the Ministry of Law, through the Directorate General of General Legal Administration (AHU) and its online apostille service.

Since 17 December 2025 the service is governed by Permenkum 50/2025 on Apostille Services, which replaced Permenkumham 6/2022. Applications are electronic. Electronically signed documents can receive an e-Apostille, and physical apostille certificates must be collected within 60 calendar days of payment.

  • Registered signature first. The signature of the official who signed your document must be registered in the AHU database. If it is not, the document must first be legalised (for example by a notary) before it can be apostilled check per document on apostille.ahu.go.id.
  • Documents that cannot be apostilled. Indonesia's declaration excludes documents issued by the Prosecutor's Office (Kejaksaan) acting as prosecutor. The Convention itself (art. 1(3)) excludes documents executed by diplomatic or consular agents, and administrative documents dealing directly with commercial or customs operations.
  • Order. The apostille usually goes on the Indonesian original (Convention art. 3). The translation and certification then follow, in Portugal.

4. What a certified translation looks like

A certified translation package usually contains:

  1. The source document (original or certified copy, apostilled where required).
  2. The translation into European Portuguese.
  3. The certificate (termo de certificação de tradução), signed and stamped by the notary or other certifying professional, bound with the translation. A certification by a lawyer, solicitor or chamber of commerce also carries its online registration number.

6. Frequently asked questions

Does Portugal have sworn translators?

No. Portugal has no official "sworn translator" profession. A translation becomes official when it is a certified translation (tradução certificada): the translator declares before a notary (Notarial Code art. 172), a lawyer, solicitor or recognised chamber of commerce (Decree-Law 237/2001 art. 5), a registry officer (Decree-Law 76-A/2006 art. 38) or the Portuguese consulate in the issuing country that it is a faithful translation.

Do Indonesian documents need an apostille to be used in Portugal?

Usually yes. Indonesia joined the Hague Apostille Convention with effect from 4 June 2022, and Portugal is also a party. Indonesian public documents with an apostille from Indonesia's Ministry of Law (AHU; Permenkum 50/2025 since 17 December 2025) no longer need embassy legalisation for use in Portugal. Prosecutor's-office, diplomatic/consular and commercial/customs documents are excluded.

Should I get the apostille before or after the translation?

Normally the apostille goes on the Indonesian original first. The document is then translated into European Portuguese and certified in Portugal. If the translation is made in Indonesia, the receiving Portuguese authority may also require an apostille on the translation. Check with the authority that will receive the document.

1. Terjemahan tersertifikasi di Portugal

Berbeda dengan Indonesia (di mana penerjemah tersumpah diatur oleh Permenkum 4/2025), Portugal tidak mengenal profesi "penerjemah tersumpah". Terjemahan diakui instansi Portugal jika berupa terjemahan tersertifikasi (tradução certificada). Penerjemah menyatakan kesesuaian terjemahan di hadapan notaris (Código do Notariado pasal 172), pengacara (advogado), solicitador atau kamar dagang yang diakui (Decreto-Lei 237/2001 pasal 5), petugas catatan sipil (Decreto-Lei 76-A/2006 pasal 38), atau konsulat Portugal di negara penerbit dokumen.

2. Dokumen yang biasanya perlu diterjemahkan

  • Akta kelahiran, akta perkawinan/buku nikah, Kartu Keluarga.
  • SKCK dari Polri. Menurut Perpol 1/2026, setiap SKCK hanya untuk satu keperluan dan satu negara tujuan, dan berlaku 6 bulan.
  • Ijazah dan transkrip nilai.
  • Akta pendirian perusahaan, NIB, dan surat kuasa.

3. Apostille

Indonesia resmi menjadi pihak Konvensi Apostille sejak 4 Juni 2022 (Perpres 2/2021), dan Portugal juga merupakan pihak. Sejak 17 Desember 2025, layanan apostille diatur oleh Permenkum 50/2025 (menggantikan Permenkumham 6/2022) dan diajukan secara elektronik melalui layanan apostille AHU. Dokumen publik Indonesia yang sudah diapostille tidak perlu lagi dilegalisasi Kedutaan Portugal.

  • Tanda tangan pejabat penanda tangan dokumen harus sudah terdaftar di basis data AHU; jika belum, dokumen harus dilegalisasi terlebih dahulu (misalnya oleh notaris). cek per dokumen
  • Tidak dapat diapostille: dokumen Kejaksaan sebagai penuntut umum (deklarasi Indonesia), serta dokumen diplomatik/konsuler dan dokumen administratif yang langsung terkait perdagangan atau kepabeanan (Konvensi pasal 1(3)).
  • Umumnya apostille dibubuhkan pada dokumen asli Indonesia, lalu dokumen diterjemahkan ke bahasa Portugis Eropa dan disertifikasi di Portugal.
