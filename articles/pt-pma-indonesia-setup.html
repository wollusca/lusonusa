1. What is a PT PMA?

A PT PMA (Perseroan Terbatas Penanaman Modal Asing) is an Indonesian limited liability company with foreign shareholding. It is the usual vehicle for foreign individuals or companies that want to trade, hire staff, invoice Indonesian customers or sponsor stay permits (KITAS) in Indonesia.

Foreign ownership: under the Investment List (Presidential Regulation 10/2021 as amended by 49/2021), many sectors, such as consulting, trading and much of tech and hospitality, allow 100% foreign ownership. Others are capped, reserved for small local businesses or closed. Always check your specific KBLI business code first.

2. Capital & investment rules (updated for 2025)

The capital rules changed with BKPM/Ministry of Investment Regulation No. 5 of 2025, in force since 2 October 2025, which replaced Regulation No. 4 of 2021:

PT PMA capital rules before and after October 2025
RuleBefore Oct 2025Since 2 Oct 2025
Minimum issued & paid-up capitalIDR 10 billionIDR 2.5 billion (approx. €130,000–140,000)
Total investment planMore than IDR 10 billion per KBLI per project location (excl. land & buildings)Unchanged: more than IDR 10 billion per KBLI per project location, unless a sector exception applies
Use of paid-up capitalNo specific holding periodReported: must stay in the company's account for at least 12 months, except for listed uses such as asset purchases, construction and operating costs verify wording

Existing PT PMAs set up with IDR 10 billion paid-up capital may be able to amend their capital structure under the new rules. Investment progress must be reported every quarter through the LKPM report in OSS.

Planning to live in Indonesia as the investor? The lower company capital does not change immigration's separate shareholding threshold for the E28A investor KITAS. See our visa guide.

3. Shareholders & management

  1. At least 2 shareholders: individuals, companies or a mix. A single founder often uses a holding company or a second shareholder.
  2. At least 1 director (Direktur): runs the company day to day. A foreign director needs an appropriate stay permit.
  3. At least 1 commissioner (Komisaris): supervises the board of directors on behalf of the shareholders.

Nominee arrangements, where an Indonesian holds shares on behalf of a foreigner, are prohibited under the Investment Law and leave the foreign investor with no legal protection.

4. OSS risk-based licensing

Business licensing runs through the Online Single Submission (OSS) system at oss.go.id. Each KBLI activity is classified as low, medium-low, medium-high or high risk:

  • NIB (Business Identification Number): the company's core registration, which also serves as its import identity.
  • Low and medium-low risk: NIB plus a self-declared standard certificate is usually enough.
  • Medium-high and high risk: the standard certificate or licence must be verified by the relevant ministry before operations start.

5. Step-by-step incorporation

  1. KBLI and structure check: confirm the business codes, foreign ownership limits and capital plan.
  2. Company name reservation: through the Ministry of Law's AHU Online system.
  3. Deed of establishment (Akta Pendirian): signed before an Indonesian notary. Foreign shareholders can sign by power of attorney, which usually needs an apostille.
  4. Ministry of Law approval (SK): legal-entity approval of the deed.
  5. Tax number (NPWP) and NIB: issued through the tax system and OSS.
  6. Bank account and capital deposit: open the corporate account and pay in the subscribed capital.

Timelines depend on the notary, the bank and the risk level of your KBLI codes. Simple, low-risk set-ups often take a few weeks from complete documents.

6. Tax & ongoing reporting

  • Corporate income tax: standard rate 22%.
  • VAT (PPN): the statutory rate is 12% since 1 January 2025. For most goods and services it is calculated on a reduced base (11/12), so the effective rate stays at 11% (MoF Regulation PMK-131/2024).
  • Monthly tax filings (withholding taxes, VAT if registered) and an annual corporate tax return.
  • Quarterly LKPM investment report through OSS.

7. Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum capital for a PT PMA in 2026?

Under BKPM/Ministry of Investment Regulation No. 5 of 2025, in force since 2 October 2025, the minimum issued and paid-up capital fell from IDR 10 billion to IDR 2.5 billion. The total investment plan must still exceed IDR 10 billion per business line (KBLI) per project location, excluding land and buildings, unless a sector exception applies.

Can a foreigner own 100% of a PT PMA?

In many sectors, yes. The Investment List opens many business lines to 100% foreign ownership, while some are capped, reserved for small businesses or closed. Check the specific KBLI code before you incorporate.

How many shareholders does a PT PMA need?

At least two shareholders (individuals or companies), plus at least one director and one commissioner.

Does setting up a PT PMA automatically give me an investor KITAS?

No. Immigration sets its own minimum shareholding for the E28A investor KITAS. A founder who incorporates at the lower IDR 2.5 billion paid-up capital may not meet that threshold.

1. O que é uma PT PMA?

Uma PT PMA (Perseroan Terbatas Penanaman Modal Asing) é uma sociedade de responsabilidade limitada indonésia com capital estrangeiro. É a estrutura habitual para empresas e investidores portugueses que querem faturar, contratar e operar na Indonésia. Muitos setores permitem 100% de capital estrangeiro, mas convém confirmar sempre o código de atividade (KBLI).

2. Regras de capital (desde outubro de 2025)

  • Capital mínimo realizado: 2,5 mil milhões IDR (antes eram 10 mil milhões IDR). Regulamento BKPM n.º 5/2025, em vigor desde 2 de outubro de 2025.
  • Plano de investimento total: continua a ter de exceder 10 mil milhões IDR por KBLI e por local de projeto, excluindo terrenos e edifícios.
  • Relatório LKPM: trimestral, através do sistema OSS.

O KITAS de investidor tem um limiar de participação próprio, definido pela imigração. Ver o guia de vistos.

3. Etapas de constituição

  1. Verificação dos códigos KBLI, da estrutura e do plano de capital.
  2. Reserva da denominação no sistema AHU do Ministério do Direito.
  3. Escritura de constituição (Akta Pendirian) perante notário indonésio.
  4. Aprovação ministerial, NPWP (número fiscal) e NIB no OSS.
  5. Abertura de conta bancária e realização do capital.
