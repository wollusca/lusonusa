1. Indonesia's visa index at a glance

Indonesia no longer uses labels such as "B211A" or "C317". Each visa has an index code under the visa classification decree Kepmen Imipas M.IP-08.GR.01.01 of 2025 (in force 2 June 2025), which replaced the 2023 index. Visit visas carry letters such as B (visa on arrival), C (single-entry visit) and D (multiple-entry visit). E codes are limited-stay visas, which become a stay permit (ITAS, commonly called KITAS) on arrival. Visas and stay permits are governed by Permenkumham 22/2023, as amended by Permenkumham 11/2024. Applications are made online through the Directorate General of Immigration's e-Visa portal.

Indonesian immigration law (Law 6/2011, as amended) separates visiting (tourism, meetings, negotiations) from working (employment or being paid by an Indonesian entity). Choose the visa that matches what you will actually do:

The visa on arrival (B1, official page) is a tourism visitor visa. Its listed purposes are travel, family visits, meetings, MICE events (meetings, incentives, conventions, exhibitions) and transit. Its official fee is IDR 500,000; an increase was proposed in August 2026 but was not in force at the time of review. Portuguese citizens are on the visa-on-arrival country list check the official country list.

2. C2 business visit visa (the replacement for the old B211A)

If you need more than the 30 days of a visa on arrival, the C2 business visit visa is the usual choice. It gives a 60-day stay and can be extended inside Indonesia by 60 days at a time, up to a total stay of 180 days (Permenkumham 22/2023, visit-stay table). An extension can be requested at the earliest 14 days before the current stay expires.

Allowed: meetings, negotiations, trade fairs, supplier and factory visits, feasibility studies. Not allowed: employment or receiving payment from an Indonesian company (Law 6/2011 art. 38 and art. 122).

What the official checklist asks for

  • A passport valid for at least 6 months.
  • A letter or invitation from the Indonesian company or government body you are visiting, explaining your relationship with them.
  • A bank statement for the last 3 months showing a balance of at least USD 2,000.

The official C2 page listed a government fee (PNBP) of IDR 2,000,000 at the time of review: IDR 1,000,000 for the visa plus IDR 1,000,000 for verification. Check the page before you apply.

3. D2 multiple-entry business visa

The D2 visa covers the same business activities as the C2 but allows multiple entries, with each stay limited to 60 days. It suits founders and buyers who travel to Indonesia several times a year. Validity periods (1 year or longer) and fees are listed on the official D2 page confirm validity options.

4. E28A investor KITAS: residence for PT PMA shareholders

If you own shares in an Indonesian foreign-investment company (PT PMA, see our PT PMA guide), the E28A investor KITAS lets you live in Indonesia and serve on the board of directors or board of commissioners of the company you invest in.

Minimum shareholding: IDR 10 billion, held personally. The Directorate General of Immigration's E28A page, which cites Permenkumham 11/2024, requires proof that you own shares worth at least IDR 10,000,000,000 in a sponsoring company registered with the Ministry of Investment/BKPM. If your shareholding is below IDR 10 billion and you are a director or commissioner, you must apply for a work visa and work KITAS instead. This immigration threshold is separate from the company's capital rules.

What the investor KITAS gives you

  • A stay permit of 1 or 2 years with re-entry, renewable while you remain a qualifying shareholder. The official fees at the time of review were IDR 7,000,000 (1 year) and IDR 9,500,000 (2 years).
  • The right to act as director or commissioner of your own PT PMA. Under manpower rules, shareholder-directors or commissioners above a certain shareholding may be exempt from the foreign-worker plan (RPTKA) and levy; confirm the current threshold with the Ministry of Manpower unconfirmed.
  • The possibility to sponsor family members (see below).
  • A route towards a permanent stay permit (KITAP) after several years (Law 6/2011 art. 54), subject to the rules in force at the time.

5. E33G remote worker visa

The E33G stay permit is for people who live in Indonesia but work for an employer or clients outside Indonesia. It is issued for one year. The official E33G page requires proof of income from abroad of at least USD 60,000 per year, an employment contract or proof of your foreign company, and a bank balance of at least USD 2,000 over the last 3 months. Extensions are filed online. Income from Indonesian sources is not allowed on this visa.

Under the Income Tax Law (art. 2(3)(a), as amended by Law 7/2021), being in Indonesia for more than 183 days in a 12-month period, or being there with the intention to reside, can make you an Indonesian tax resident. Get tax advice from a qualified tax consultant before you move.

6. Family members

A foreign KITAS holder can sponsor family members on a family-reunification stay permit, which follows the main holder's validity. For the spouse of an ITAS/ITAP holder the code is E31B (1 or 2 years; official fees IDR 6,000,000 or 8,500,000 at the time of review). For a child of an ITAS/ITAP holder it is E31E. (E31A is for spouses of Indonesian citizens.) Marriage and birth certificates issued in Portugal usually need an apostille and a translation.

7. How the application works

  1. Check your purpose and the checklist: we go through your plans and the official document checklist with you and help you identify the right visa.
  2. Sponsor and online filing: your sponsor (for example your Indonesian business partner or your PT PMA) and you complete the application on the e-Visa portal.
  3. Approval: the e-Visa is sent by email. You do not need to visit the Indonesian Embassy in Lisbon for most e-Visas.
  4. After arrival (KITAS only): you may need to give biometrics at an immigration office before the stay permit is issued; practice varies by office.

Processing times vary by visa type and by immigration office. Allow extra time around Indonesian public holidays.

8. Common compliance pitfalls

  • Working on a visit visa. This can lead to deportation and an entry ban (Law 6/2011 art. 75 and art. 122).
  • Overstaying. For an overstay of less than 60 days, the charge applied in practice is IDR 1,000,000 per day; not paying leads to deportation and an entry ban. Overstaying for more than 60 days leads to deportation and an entry ban (Law 6/2011 art. 78). The fee regulation changed in August 2026 (PP 30/2026), so check the current amount with immigration fee basis unconfirmed.
  • Not reporting changes. KITAS holders must report changes of address, employer or civil status to immigration (Law 6/2011 art. 71).
  • Mismatched sponsor. Your sponsor and the activity on your visa must match what you actually do in Indonesia.

9. Frequently asked questions

Does the B211A visa still exist?

No. Indonesia now labels its visas with index codes. For a single-entry business visit, the C2 business visit visa replaced the old B211A business route. You apply for it online at evisa.imigrasi.go.id.

Can Portuguese citizens get a visa on arrival in Indonesia?

Yes. Portuguese passport holders can use the visa on arrival (index B1, also available online as e-VoA) for a 30-day stay, extendable once. Its official purposes are travel, family visits, meetings, MICE events and transit. It does not allow work.

Can I work in Indonesia on a C2 or D2 business visa?

No. Business visit visas allow meetings, negotiations, market research and similar activities, but not employment or receiving payment from an Indonesian company. Working requires a work-based stay permit (KITAS).

What is the E28A investor KITAS?

E28A is the investor stay permit for foreigners who invest in an Indonesian company, usually a PT PMA. It allows them to sit on the board of directors or commissioners of that company. The official E28A page (Permenkumham 11/2024) requires a personal shareholding of at least IDR 10 billion; a director or commissioner with a smaller shareholding needs a work KITAS instead.

1. Os vistos em resumo

A Indonésia deixou de usar designações como "B211A". Cada visto tem agora um código de índice (Kepmen Imipas M.IP-08.GR.01.01/2025) e o pedido é feito online no portal e-Visa da Direção-Geral de Imigração.

  • Visto à chegada (B1, e-VoA): 30 dias, prorrogável uma vez. Finalidades oficiais: turismo, visitas familiares, reuniões, eventos MICE e trânsito.
  • C2, visto de visita de negócios: entrada única, 60 dias, prorrogável na Indonésia por períodos de 60 dias até 180 dias no total (Permenkumham 22/2023). Substitui o antigo B211A para fins de negócios.
  • D2: visto de negócios de entradas múltiplas para viagens frequentes.
  • E28A, KITAS de investidor: autorização de estadia para sócios de uma PT PMA.
  • E33G, trabalhador remoto: 1 ano, para quem trabalha para entidades fora da Indonésia (rendimento mínimo de 60 000 USD/ano).

Nenhum visto de visita permite trabalhar ou receber pagamentos de uma entidade indonésia.

2. KITAS de investidor (E28A)

Permite residir na Indonésia e integrar a administração ou o conselho de comissários da sua PT PMA. A página oficial E28A (Permenkumham 11/2024) exige uma participação pessoal de pelo menos 10 mil milhões IDR numa empresa registada no BKPM. Com uma participação inferior, um administrador ou comissário tem de pedir um KITAS de trabalho. Este limiar é distinto das regras de capital da empresa.

3. Como funciona o pedido

  1. Analisamos consigo o objetivo da viagem e a lista oficial de documentos.
  2. O seu patrocinador (parceiro indonésio ou a sua PT PMA) e o requerente submetem o pedido no portal e-Visa.
  3. O e-Visa é enviado por email. Na maioria dos casos não é necessário ir à Embaixada da Indonésia em Lisboa.
  4. No caso de KITAS, pode ser necessário recolher dados biométricos num serviço de imigração após a chegada.
