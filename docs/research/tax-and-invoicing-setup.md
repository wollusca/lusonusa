*Research date: 26 October 2026. Prepared for: Aditya Putra (founder) and the three Portugal-based professionals.*

> **This is research, not tax or legal advice.** It summarises public primary and secondary sources as found on the research date. Thresholds and rates change every budget year. Anything marked **[unconfirmed]** could not be checked against the official text, and some primary sites (diariodarepublica.pt, pajak.go.id) could not be opened directly from the research environment. In those cases the citation points to the official page and the content was checked through secondary summaries. ## 0. Start here: one-page summary

### Recommended model for the trial: "everyone invoices their own work, under their own name"

1. **Each of the three professionals** opens activity at Finanças and issues **recibos verdes directly to the client**, whether the client is in Portugal, in Indonesia or elsewhere. LUSONUSA is only a brand and a referral network. It never invoices.
2. **Aditya invoices work that he performs from Portugal with his own Portuguese recibo verde**, not through PT WSB. As a Portuguese tax resident working from Lisbon, he would be the main risk if that work went through PT WSB (see B6).
3. **PT WSB invoices only work that is actually performed in or from Indonesia** and fits its KBLI codes, such as local Indonesian tourism or travel-agent services and consulting delivered by people in Indonesia. During the trial, PT WSB should **not** invoice for the other three professionals.
4. **Network fee and referral cut:** Aditya invoices each professional with a simple **Portuguese recibo verde** (for example, a monthly network fee plus a referral commission). Do not route these through PT WSB. Back this with a **2–3 page written network agreement**.
5. **Revisit the model** when any of these happens: one person nears **€15,000** of annual turnover (VAT and withholding limits); Indonesian clients reach roughly **20–30% of revenue** or there is a recurring B2B client in Indonesia; someone (including Aditya) relocates or spends **183 days or more** in the other country; or PT WSB acquires real staff or an office in Indonesia.

**Why:** Model (iii), where everything for Indonesian clients goes through PT WSB, triggers **Indonesian 20% PPh 26 withholding** on every payment to the Portugal-based professionals, unless a DGT form and residence certificate are prepared for each one. It also triggers **self-assessed Indonesian VAT (about 11% effective) on imported services**, monthly Coretax filings, and **corporate tax at standard rates**. A PT newly registered in 2026 can no longer use the 0.5% final tax (PP 20/2026). On top of that, it raises **Portuguese residence and permanent-establishment questions** for PT WSB, because its only manager works from Lisbon. For tiny trial revenue, this administrative load and risk is out of proportion.

## A. Portugal: the three self-employed professionals (and Aditya for his Portuguese work)

### A1. Opening or changing activity (início / alteração de atividade)

**Legal basis**
- Art. 112.º CIRS: an individual must declare the start of a Category B activity before starting it. Art. 31.º CIVA covers the VAT side. A late or missing declaration is fined under the RGIT; secondary sources cite **€600 to €7,500** [unconfirmed against RGIT art. 118]. Sources: [CIRS on Portal das Finanças](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/codigos_tributarios/irs/Pages/codigo-do-irs.aspx); [Montepio guide](https://www.montepio.org/ei/pessoal/impostos/abrir-atividade-no-portal-das-financas-saiba-tudo-neste-guia-completo/).
- Activity codes come from the **table of activities in art. 151.º CIRS** (Portaria 1011/2001, [DR I-B n.º 193, 21 Aug 2001](https://files.dre.pt/1s/2001/08/193b00/53905391.pdf); [AT page for art. 151.º](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/codigos_tributarios/irs/Pages/irs155.aspx)) and from the **CAE Rev.4** classification (DL 9/2025, in force since 1 Jan 2025; [AT note on CAE validation](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/destaques/Paginas/Validacao_CAE.aspx)).

**Codes that fit**

| Service | CIRS code (art. 151 table) | Coefficient / withholding effect | CAE (pick from the AT list) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Certified translation PT↔ID | **1334 – Tradutores** | 0.75 coefficient; 23% withholding | Translation and interpretation, CAE **74300** (Rev.3), mapped to Rev.4 code 74.30 [unconfirmed] |
| In-person accompaniment to public offices and banks; banking and business guidance | **1519 – Outros prestadores de serviços** (no specific code exists for "consultants" or "accompaniment") | 0.35 coefficient; 11.5% withholding | Management consulting (CAE 70220 in Rev.3, 70200 in Rev.4 [unconfirmed]) or other business support services (82990) [unconfirmed] |
| Interpreting / guide work (optional) | 1326 – Guias-intérpretes | 0.75 coefficient | — |

There is **no specific CIRS code for "consultor"**. The residual code **1519** is the usual choice ([search summary of the art. 151.º table](https://informador.pt/legislacao/lexit/codigos/direito-fiscal/codigo-do-irs/legislacao-complementar/tabela-de-actividades-do-artigo-151-o-do-cirs/anexo-i/); [Doutor Finanças on CIRS vs CAE](https://www.doutorfinancas.pt/carreira-e-rendimentos/trabalhadores-independentes/codigo-da-lista-cirs-151-ou-cae-como-e-qual-escolher/)).

**Several activities.** Yes, one person can have several. The declaration has one main CAE plus secondary CAEs, and one main CIRS code plus secondary ones. Secondary sources cite up to 19 secondary CAEs and 4 secondary CIRS codes [unconfirmed]. Each recibo verde is issued under the specific code for that service, which determines the coefficient and withholding rate for that income.

**Practical steps (free online)**
1. Portal das Finanças → *Todos os Serviços* → *Início de Atividade* → *Entregar declaração*. You need NIF and password, or Chave Móvel Digital. Online filing is free; a paper form costs €0.35.
2. Give the expected annual turnover (keep it **≤ €15,000** to fall within art. 53), choose the **VAT regime "isenção art. 53.º"**, choose the **simplified regime**, and enter the CIRS and CAE codes.
3. AT forwards the data to the Segurança Social automatically.
4. Then issue every invoice as a *fatura-recibo* (recibo verde) on the Portal.
5. If someone already has an open activity, they file an *alteração de atividade* to add codes. This is also free.

**Cost and effort:** €0, about 30 minutes. **Rating: CRITICAL.**

### A2. VAT (IVA)

**Art. 53 CIVA small-business exemption**
- The threshold is **€15,000 of annual turnover in Portuguese territory in the previous calendar year**. It rose in steps under the 2023 State Budget: €13,500 in 2023, €14,500 in 2024, and €15,000 from 2025 ([OCC study](https://occ.pt/sites/default/files/public/2025-03/pequenasA1.pdf); [art. 53.º on Portal das Finanças](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/codigos_tributarios/civa_rep/Pages/artigo-53-o-do-civa.aspx)). It remains €15,000 in 2026.
- **DL 35/2025 of 24 March 2025** transposed the EU SME scheme (Directive (EU) 2020/285, applicable from 1 Jan 2025). Changes ([DL 35/2025 text, PGDL](https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?artigo_id=3904A0001&nid=3904&tabela=leis&pagina=1&ficha=1&so_miolo=&nversao=); [OCC news](https://occ.pt/pt-pt/noticias/iva-isencoes-16)):
  - Having, or being required to have, organised accounting no longer blocks the exemption.
  - **Exit rule:** if turnover goes over €15,000 in the current year by **more than 25%** (that is, above **€18,750**), the exemption ends **immediately**, starting with the invoice that crosses the line. If turnover lands between €15,000 and €18,750, the exemption lasts until 31 December and VAT applies from the following year. A change declaration is due within 15 working days.
  - **Cross-border SME scheme:** an EU-established small business can use exemptions in other EU Member States if its EU-wide turnover is **≤ €100,000**, after prior notification and with an "EX" suffix on its VAT number ([Vendus](https://www.vendus.pt/blog/isento-de-iva-artigo-53/)) [unconfirmed against official text]. This is not relevant while the professionals only supply services from Portugal.
  - The exemption is still unavailable to anyone who carries out **exports or related activities**, a condition that concerns goods. Services to non-EU clients that are not located in Portugal are not "exports" in the art. 14 CIVA sense [unconfirmed; ask the contabilista].
- Practical effect: no VAT is charged and no periodic VAT returns are filed, with the exceptions below.

**Place of supply (art. 6.º CIVA) and what each recibo verde should say**

| Client | Rule | Taxed in Portugal? | Wording on the recibo verde |
|---|---|---|---|

| (b) Indonesian individual (non-EU, not a business) | General rule: Portugal, especially for physical services such as accompaniment in Lisbon. **Exception, art. 6.º n.º 9:** consultancy-type services (consultants, lawyers, economists, information supply, etc.) supplied to non-taxable persons outside the EU are **not taxed in Portugal**. Whether translation counts as such a service is **[unconfirmed]**. | Accompaniment: yes, exempt under art. 53. Banking/business guidance: probably not located in Portugal under art. 6.º n.º 9 [unconfirmed]. | Under art. 53, simplest to use "IVA – regime de isenção (art. 53.º)". Ask the contabilista whether advisory items should say "Não sujeito – art. 6.º n.º 9 CIVA". Sources: [OCC on location](https://www.occ.pt/pt-pt/noticias/iva-localizacao-das-prestacoes-de-servicos-17); [ISG paper](https://www.isg.pt/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/46_1_marta-almeida_-iva-localizacao-ps_f46.pdf) |
| (c) Indonesian company (B2B) | Art. 6.º n.º 6 a): taxed where the **customer** is established, so Indonesia | No | "IVA – autoliquidação" or "Não sujeito – art. 6.º n.º 6 a) do CIVA". Use-and-enjoyment rule, art. 6.º n.º 12 [unconfirmed scope]. |

**Watch out for the reverse charge on foreign purchases.** A person exempt under art. 53 who **buys** services from a supplier outside Portugal (for example, a fee invoice from PT WSB, a foreign website platform, or Google or Meta ads) may have to **self-assess 23% Portuguese VAT** under art. 2.º n.º 1 i) CIVA and file a periodic return for that month, without the right to deduct ([AT binding ruling PIV 27404](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/informacoes_vinculativas/despesa/civa/Documents/PIV_27404.pdf); [PIV 29777](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/informacoes_vinculativas/despesa/civa/Documents/PIV_29777.pdf)). This is a key reason **not** to have PT WSB invoice the network fee to the Portuguese professionals (see C).

**Rating: CRITICAL** (correct regime and wording from the first recibo).

### A3. IRS (Category B)

**Simplified regime (art. 28.º and art. 31.º CIRS)** ([art. 31.º annotated, Portal das Finanças](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/codigos_tributarios/cirs_rep/ra/Pages/irs31ra_202503.aspx); [OCC](https://occ.pt/pt-pt/noticias/irs-regime-simplificado-4))
- It applies automatically while Category B income is **≤ €200,000** (art. 28.º n.º 2).
- Coefficients (art. 31.º n.º 1):
  - **0.75** for professions in the **art. 151.º table**, so translation (1334) is 75% taxable.
  - **0.35** for other services (1519), so accompaniment and guidance is 35% taxable.
  - (0.15 applies to sales and hospitality; not relevant here.)
- **First years (art. 31.º n.º 10):** the coefficients in sub-paragraphs b), c) and f) are **reduced by 50% in the year activity starts and by 25% in the following year**. This applies only if the person has **no Category A (employment) or H (pension) income** in those years, and not if an activity ceased less than 5 years earlier (n.º 11). In the start year, translation would be taxed at 0.375 and other services at 0.175.
- **15% expense rule (art. 31.º n.º 13), for the 0.75 coefficient only:** 15% of gross income must be backed by expenses and charges (staff costs, rents, other e-fatura expenses linked to the activity, and the specific deduction counted toward it). Otherwise the shortfall is added to taxable income [unconfirmed wording; ask the contabilista]. **Practical step:** request invoices with your NIF for the phone, internet, transport, a share of rent, software and training, and mark them as professional in e-fatura.
- Income goes into IRS **Anexo B**. Foreign tax withheld goes into **Anexo J**, with a credit under art. 81.º CIRS and art. 23 of the PT–ID treaty.

**Withholding at source (retenção na fonte), art. 101.º CIRS**
- **Who must withhold:** only payers that **have, or must have, organised accounting** (art. 101.º n.º 1). In practice:
  - **Portuguese company or Portuguese business with accounting:** must withhold **23%** for art. 151 activities (code 1334). The rate was reduced from 25% by Lei 45-A/2024, OE2025 ([OCC](https://occ.pt/pt-pt/noticias/taxa-de-retencao-dos-recibos-verdes-baixou-para-23-mas-pode-optar-se-por-25)). For other services (code 1519) the rate is **11.5%** ([DECO](https://www.deco.proteste.pt/dinheiro/impostos/dicas/retencao-fonte-recibos-verdes)).
  - **Private individuals (not acting as a business):** no withholding.
  - **Foreign companies (Indonesia, PT WSB, etc.) with no establishment in Portugal:** no Portuguese withholding. On the recibo, choose "sem retenção". Indonesian withholding may apply instead; see B7.
- **Dispensa (art. 101.º-B n.º 1 a)):** a person whose Category B income in the previous year, and expected income in the current year, is **below €15,000** may choose not to be withheld on. The recibo must state: *"Sem retenção – n.º 1 do art. 101.º-B do CIRS"*. The dispensa ends from the month after the €15,000 limit is reached ([Vendus](https://www.vendus.pt/blog/dispensa-retencao-fonte/)). It is optional, and it does not cover intermediation commissions (art. 101.º-B n.º 2). That matters for referral commissions; see C.
- **Advance payments (pagamentos por conta, art. 102.º CIRS)** may follow from the second year if tax due is high enough [unconfirmed 2026 parameters].

**Rating:** codes and wording **CRITICAL**; expense tracking **SHOULD DO SOON**.

### A4. Segurança Social (independent workers)

Main law: **Código dos Regimes Contributivos (CRC), Lei 110/2009** ([consolidated text, DR](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/legislacao-consolidada/lei/2009-34514575-117658277)); [DGSS information sheet](https://sisscontent.seg-social.pt/documents/10152/113014/folha_informativa.pdf/3190c94f-2c7a-4d12-b123-94a849917ef8); [seg-social.pt FAQ](https://www.seg-social.pt/ptss/pssd/documento/cmdddzytm006agq2y1ci6tibc).

- **First-time exemption:** the first enrolment takes effect on the **first day of the 12th month after the activity starts** (art. 164.º CRC [article number unconfirmed]), so in practice there are about 12 months with no contributions. Early enrolment can be requested in a quarterly declaration. Someone who was self-employed before only gets the unused remainder.
- **Rate:** **21.4%** (art. 168.º CRC) on the **relevant income**. For services, relevant income is **70% of the income of the previous quarter** (art. 162.º CRC), which works out to an effective 14.98% of gross. A minimum contribution of €20 per month applies when there is income [unconfirmed]. You can adjust the base by ±25% in the declaration.
- **Quarterly declaration (art. 151.º–152.º CRC):** filed on Segurança Social Direta by the **end of January, April, July and October**, covering the previous 3 months. The DGSS sheet says the declaration is required even during the exemption; some sources disagree, so confirm in SS Direta. Once enrolled, contributions are paid monthly, between the 10th and the 20th.
- **Also employed (art. 157.º n.º 1 a) CRC):** the worker is **exempt** from independent contributions if all of the following apply:
  1. The employer is a different entity, with no group or control relationship with the self-employed clients.
  2. The employment carries full social protection.
  3. Average monthly employment pay is ≥ **1 × IAS (€537.13 in 2026)**.
  4. Average monthly relevant self-employed income is < **4 × IAS (€2,148.52 in 2026)**.
  
  IAS 2026 is per Portaria 480-A/2025/1 [unconfirmed number] ([OCC practical guide, Aug 2026](https://www.occ.pt/sites/default/files/public/2026-08/Guia_pratico_ago_2026a.pdf); [art. 157.º text](https://informador.pt/legislacao/lexit/codigos/direito-fiscal/codigo-dos-regimes-contributivos-do-sistema-previdencial-de-seguranca-social/parte-ii-regimes-contributivos-do-sistema-previdencial/titulo-ii-regime-dos-trabalhadores-independentes/capitulo-iii-relacao-juridica-contributiva/seccao-i-obrigacoes-dos-contribuintes/artigo-157-o-isencao-da-obrigacao-de-contribuir/)). The exemption must be **checked and, where needed, requested**. Having it does not remove the obligation to be registered.
- **Contracting-entity rule (entidade contratante, art. 140.º CRC):** if one client pays **≥ 50%** of a professional's annual income, that client may owe a 7–10% contribution. For a foreign client such as PT WSB, how this is enforced is **[unconfirmed]**. It is another reason not to have most income flow through one payer.

**Rating: SHOULD DO SOON.** Note the first declaration window after opening, and make sure each person's SS Direta access works.

### A5. Payments from abroad, and currency

- **No special tax declaration** is needed to receive a transfer from Indonesia. Every payment must be backed by a recibo verde **issued at the time of payment**. A fatura-recibo is issued on the payment date; if payment comes later, use a fatura and then a recibo.
- **Currency:** the recibo verde is in EUR. If the client pays in IDR or USD, convert at the rate on the payment date and keep the bank statement [unconfirmed whether the portal accepts other currencies; CIVA art. 36.º n.º 5 requires amounts in EUR or the VAT amount to be stated in EUR].
- **Foreign bank accounts** (Wise, an Indonesian account, etc.) must be listed in IRS **Anexo J, quadro 11** [unconfirmed quadro number].
- **Banco de Portugal statistical reporting (COPE)** on operations with non-residents is aimed at larger amounts and businesses. Trial-size flows are unlikely to trigger it [unconfirmed]. **CAN WAIT.**
- Indonesian clients may need to show an invoice to their bank for outgoing transfers. A recibo verde with the client's passport or NPWP number usually works [unconfirmed].
- Fees: a SEPA or SWIFT receipt from Indonesia often costs €10–25. A multi-currency account such as Wise is cheaper. This is a cost issue, not a tax issue.

## B. The founder in Portugal and PT WSB

### B6. Risks of invoicing work done in Portugal through PT WSB

Plain-language picture: **in tax terms, where a company "lives" depends on where it is actually run and where the work is done, not where it is registered.** Aditya is PT WSB's only real decision-maker and lives in Lisbon. If PT WSB's income comes from work he does in Lisbon, Portugal has several ways to tax it.

| Risk | Legal basis | How serious | How likely at micro scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PT WSB treated as Portuguese tax resident** (place of effective management) | Art. 2.º n.º 3 CIRC: a company is resident if its head office **or effective management** is in Portugal. **Treaty art. 4(3):** a dual-resident company is resident only where its **place of effective management** is ([treaty text](https://s3-ap-northeast-1.amazonaws.com/dsa-library/Resources/2690/ASEAN_Indonesia_Portugal_DTA.pdf)). | High in principle: Portuguese IRC on **all** of PT WSB's profits at **19% in 2026** (Lei 64/2025; 15% on the first €50k for SMEs) ([PwC OE2026](https://www.pwc.pt/pt/pwcinforfisco/orcamentoestado/irc.html)), plus Portuguese accounting, a TOC and IRC returns. | Low-to-medium while revenue is tiny, because audits target larger amounts. The risk grows quickly if PT WSB's revenue is mostly Aditya's Lisbon work. |
| **Portuguese permanent establishment (PE)** | Art. 5.º CIRC. Treaty art. 5(1) (fixed place of business, such as a home office used regularly), **art. 5(3)(b)** (services by personnel for **more than 183 days in any 12 months**), art. 5(5) (dependent agent habitually concluding contracts). | Profits attributable to the PE are taxed in Portugal (art. 7). | Medium if Aditya works for PT WSB clients from Lisbon most of the year. The 183-day services test would be met. |
| **His own IRS on worldwide income** | Art. 15.º CIRS: a resident is taxed on worldwide income. Fees, salary or dividends from PT WSB are taxable in Portugal (treaty arts. 14, 15, 10, 23). Directors' fees may also be taxed in Indonesia (art. 16), with a credit in Portugal. | Normal IRS. Not a "risk" as such, but it must be declared (Anexos B/A/E/J). | Certain, if he takes money out. |
| **CFC / imputation of PT WSB profits to him** | Art. 66.º CIRC, which also applies to individuals via art. 20.º CIRS. It applies when Aditya holds ≥ 25% and PT WSB pays **< 50% of the IRC that would be due in Portugal**, or the country is on the blacklist (Portaria 150/2004 — Indonesia is not on it [unconfirmed]) ([art. 66 on Portal das Finanças](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/codigos_tributarios/CIRC_2R/Pages/irc66.aspx)). | PT WSB's profit is taxed in Aditya's IRS each year even if it is not distributed. | **Low** under standard Indonesian PPh Badan: 22%, or 11% effective with art. 31E, compared with the 50% × 19% = 9.5% test. It **would be higher if PT WSB paid only 0.5% turnover tax**, but that option is closed to new PTs (see B7). The substance and passive-income conditions in art. 66 may also exclude it [unconfirmed]. |
| **Anti-abuse / treaty PPT** | General anti-avoidance rule, art. 38.º n.º 2 LGT. MLI principal-purpose test: the Indonesia–Portugal treaty appears in Indonesia's list of MLI-modified treaties ([pajak.go.id synthesised text](https://www.pajak.go.id/sites/default/files/2025-06/Tax%20Treaty%20Portugal%20(Modified%20by%20MLI)%20-%20EN.pdf)) [PPT wording unconfirmed]. | Recharacterisation as Aditya's own Portuguese income. | Low at micro scale, but routing Portuguese work through a low-substance foreign company looks bad. |
| **Unregistered Portuguese activity** | Art. 112.º CIRS / RGIT. Doing professional work in Portugal without an open activity. | Fines plus back taxes. | Avoidable: Aditya opens activity in Portugal (A1). |

**Bottom line:** for the trial, **Aditya should invoice work he does from Portugal on his own Portuguese recibo verde.** PT WSB should only bill work with real Indonesian substance: done in Indonesia, by people in Indonesia, or for an Indonesian travel or tourism activity under its KBLI. Also note that **translation is not among PT WSB's KBLI codes**. The 2020 code for translation and interpretation is **74300**, mapped to **74901** in KBLI 2025 ([kbli.co.id](https://kbli.co.id/74300)) [unconfirmed via BPS/OSS]. PT WSB invoicing certified translation would also be a licensing mismatch. **Rating: CRITICAL.**

### B7. Indonesian side: PT WSB

**Corporate income tax regime**
- **PP 55/2022** allowed a PT a **0.5% final tax on turnover** for at most **3 tax years**, if turnover was ≤ Rp 4.8 bn (art. 57–59 PP 55/2022; [DJP press release](https://pajak.go.id/sites/default/files/2022-12/SP-68%202022%20Pemerintah%20Sesuaikan%20Aturan%20Pajak%20Penghasilan.pdf)).
- **PP 20/2026**, signed and in force **22 April 2026**, amended PP 55/2022. The 0.5% final tax is now limited to **individuals, PT Perorangan and cooperatives**. **A CV, firm or ordinary PT newly registered can no longer use it**; only those already using it keep it until their period ends (Pasal II angka 1 huruf e) ([DJP article](https://www.pajak.go.id/en/node/119950); [DDTC](https://news.ddtc.co.id/berita/nasional/1819744/pp-berlaku-pph-final-umkm-kini-bukan-untuk-cv-pt-firma-dan-bumdes); [PKN STAN](https://taxcenterpknstan.com/pp-20-2026/)) [read the official text on peraturan.bpk.go.id / jdih.kemenkeu.go.id].
- **PT WSB (NIB mid-2026) is therefore most likely on standard PPh Badan at 22%** (art. 17 UU PPh, as amended by UU 7/2021 HPP), with the **art. 31E UU PPh 50% rate reduction** on the portion of taxable profit from turnover up to Rp 4.8 bn (turnover ≤ Rp 50 bn), which works out to about **11% effective on net profit** ([DDTC on 31E](https://news.ddtc.co.id/berita/nasional/1802253/diskon-tarif-pasal-31e-uu-pph-tak-ada-batas-waktu-asal-penuhi-kriteria)).
  - **Exception:** if PT WSB is actually a **PT Perorangan** (single founder), it may still use the 0.5% tax [confirm the company type].
  - This means bookkeeping and an annual SPT PPh Badan through Coretax, plus monthly instalments (PPh 25) [confirm].
- **Indonesian VAT (PPN):** PT WSB is a small business and does not need to register as PKP while turnover stays **≤ Rp 4.8 bn** (PMK 197/2013 as amended by PMK 164/2023) ([DDTC](https://news.ddtc.co.id/berita/nasional/1810885/omzet-belum-tembus-rp48-miliar-bolehkah-pengusaha-kecil-pungut-ppn)). So no PPN is charged on its own sales.

**PPh 26 when PT WSB pays the Portugal-based professionals**
- **Domestic rule:** art. 26(1)(c) UU PPh requires **20% final withholding on gross** fees for services paid to non-residents. In practice, DJP treats the Indonesian payer as the source even when the service is performed abroad ([Ortax on PPh 26](https://ortax.org/pph-pasal-26-subjek-objek-tarif-dan-kaitannya-dengan-tax-treaty)) [nuance unconfirmed].
- **Treaty relief.** Under the Indonesia–Portugal Agreement (signed in Lisbon on **9 July 2003**, approved in Portugal by **Resolução da AR n.º 64/2006**, DR I n.º 234 of 6 Dec 2006, effective in Indonesia from **1 Jan 2008** ([pajak.go.id treaty page](https://pajak.go.id/en/p3b/portuguese-republic); [treaty text](https://s3-ap-northeast-1.amazonaws.com/dsa-library/Resources/2690/ASEAN_Indonesia_Portugal_DTA.pdf))):
  - **Art. 14 (Independent personal services):** income of a Portuguese-resident individual is taxable **only in Portugal**. The exceptions are a fixed base regularly available in Indonesia, or presence in Indonesia of **≥ 183 days in any 12-month period**. The three professionals work in Portugal, so **Indonesian tax is reduced to 0%**.
  - **Art. 7 (Business profits):** gives the same result for enterprises without an Indonesian PE.
  - **Art. 12 (Royalties):** a 10% cap. Not relevant to services, unless a translation was licensed as copyright.
- **Formal conditions for 0%:** **PMK 112/2025**, in force about 30–31 Dec 2025, replaced PER-25/PJ/2018 ([PMK 112/2025 on JDIH Kemenkeu](https://jdih.kemenkeu.go.id/dok/pmk-112-tahun-2025/summary); [DJP implementation note](https://www.pajak.go.id/en/node/118936); [Ortax on the new DGT form](https://ortax.org/format-baru-dgt-form-di-pmk-112-2025-apa-saja-yang-berubah)). Each professional must:
  1. Complete the **new-format DGT Form**.
  2. Have it certified by AT, or attach an **English-language Portuguese certificate of tax residence**. This can be requested free on the Portal das Finanças: *Certidões → Certificado de residência fiscal* (treaty version).
  3. Give it to PT WSB **before the payment / withholding date**.
  
  PT WSB must then still issue a **bukti potong** (BP26/BPNR in Coretax e-Bupot Unifikasi) at 0% and file the **SPT Masa PPh Unifikasi by the 20th of the following month** ([DDTC on BPNR](https://news.ddtc.co.id/literasi/tips-trik/1820840/cara-buat-bukti-potong-non-resident-bpnr-di-coretax-djp)). The withholding agent now carries more responsibility for checking the form. **Without the form, 20% must be withheld** and paid by the 15th of the next month. Portugal would give the professional a credit for it only up to the Portuguese tax on that income (treaty art. 23(1)(a); art. 81.º CIRS). Because the coefficients are low, part of the 20% would usually be lost.
- **PPh 26 on payments to Aditya:** Aditya is an Indonesian national but a Portuguese resident, so the same rules apply. **Directors' fees (art. 16) remain taxable in Indonesia.** Dividends are capped at 10% under the treaty. PMK 112/2025 adds a 365-day holding rule for dividends [unconfirmed detail]. Aditya should also check his own **Indonesian tax status**: an Indonesian NPWP may still show him as a resident, so he should declare non-resident status. Ask the consultant.

**Indonesian VAT on imported services (PPN JLN)**
- If PT WSB **buys** services from abroad that are used in Indonesia, it must **self-assess PPN even as a non-PKP**: 12% × DPP 11/12, about **11% effective** (PMK 131/2024). It is paid by SSP or billing code **by the 15th of the next month**, under PMK 81/2024 art. 94 ([Ortax PPN JLN](https://ortax.org/ppn-jln); [Ortax calculator](https://tools.ortax.org/ppn-jasa-luar-negeri)).
- A non-PKP **cannot credit** this VAT, so it is a **pure cost of about 11%** on everything PT WSB buys from the Portugal-based professionals.
- Whether services physically performed in Lisbon for an Indonesian client count as "utilised in Indonesia" is arguable. A translation delivered to Indonesia clearly is [unconfirmed].

**Transfer pricing / related parties**
- Aditya controls PT WSB, so payments between them, and any arrangement where PT WSB keeps a margin while he does the work, must be **arm's length** (art. 18(3) UU PPh; Portugal art. 63.º CIRC).
- Indonesian TP documentation (PMK 172/2023) only kicks in at much higher turnover or transaction values [unconfirmed thresholds]. **CAN WAIT**, but keep simple written fee terms.

**Rating:** standard Indonesian filings for PT WSB are **CRITICAL** (nil returns too). PPh 26, DGT and PPN JLN are **CRITICAL only if PT WSB pays foreigners**, which the recommended model avoids.

### B8. Comparing the three models

| | (i) Each professional invoices the client directly | (ii) PT WSB invoices only Aditya's own work | (iii) Everything for Indonesian clients goes through PT WSB |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portuguese side | Recibo verde, art. 53, simplified regime. Already needed anyway. | Same for the three. Aditya's Lisbon work through PT WSB raises PE and effective-management risk (B6). | Same as (ii), plus the professionals depend on one foreign payer (contracting-entity rule; "PT WSB as intermediary" VAT question). |
| Indonesian side | Nothing. Indonesian individual clients pay abroad. Indonesian **company** clients may withhold PPh 26 at 20% unless given a DGT form. That is the client's job; the professional supplies the DGT form and residence certificate. | PT WSB taxes on Aditya's fees. If he does the work from Lisbon, Indonesia's taxing right is weak while Portugal's is strong, so profit could be taxed twice and argued over. | **20% PPh 26 or a DGT pack for each professional**, monthly BPNR filings, **about 11% PPN JLN cost**, PPh Badan on PT WSB's margin, and the KBLI mismatch for translation. |
| Admin per month | Very low | Medium | High |
| Tax leakage | Minimal | Possible double taxation | 11% VAT cost, plus 20% if the paperwork fails |
| Client experience | Several invoices (one per professional). Can be presented under one LUSONUSA quote. | — | One IDR invoice from an Indonesian entity. This is the one real advantage. |
| **Verdict for the trial** | **Recommended** | Only for work genuinely done in or from Indonesia | **Not recommended now** |

**Recommended model:** (i) for all four. Aditya uses his Portuguese recibo verde for his Portuguese-performed work. PT WSB is kept for genuine Indonesian activity (tourism, travel and local consulting delivered in Indonesia). To give Indonesian clients a one-stop experience, LUSONUSA can issue a single **non-fiscal quote / engagement letter** listing each professional's part. The client then pays each professional's recibo verde, or a payment link per professional.

**When to revisit:**
- A professional approaches **€15,000 per year**, which brings VAT registration, withholding changes and possibly a move to a single Portuguese company (Lda / Unipessoal).
- Indonesian **corporate** clients become regular, and need IDR invoices plus PPh 23/26 handling on their side.
- PT WSB gets staff or an office in Indonesia, so there is real substance.
- Total network revenue passes about **€40–50k per year**, the usual point where a Portuguese company with a contabilista becomes cheaper than four sets of personal compliance [rule of thumb, not a legal threshold].
- Anyone changes residence or spends **183 days or more** in the other country.

## C. Network fee and referral cut

**Simplest compliant flow**
1. **Who invoices:** Aditya, with his own Portuguese activity open, issues a **recibo verde to each professional**. Use CIRS 1519, with a secondary CAE for business support or advertising services [pick with the contabilista]. Two lines are enough:
   - "Taxa de rede / serviços de plataforma e website LUSONUSA – [mês]"
   - "Comissão de angariação de cliente – [ref. cliente]"
   
   LUSONUSA is only a brand, not a legal person, so it cannot invoice.
2. **VAT:** Aditya is exempt under art. 53, so no VAT is charged and the professionals have nothing to self-assess.
3. **Withholding:** the professionals are in the simplified regime without organised accounting, so they are **not required to withhold** (art. 101.º n.º 1). Aditya receives the full amount. The art. 101.º-B dispensa excludes intermediation commissions; this only matters if a payer with accounting ever pays him.
4. **IRS for Aditya:** Category B income at the 0.35 coefficient.
5. **IRS for the payers:** the fee is a business expense. It counts toward the 15% justified-expense rule for the 0.75 translators. In the simplified regime it does not reduce tax directly beyond that rule.
6. **Segurança Social:** the fee counts in Aditya's relevant income. For the payers it is not deductible from the 70% base.
7. **Why not PT WSB?** If PT WSB (a non-EU supplier) invoices the Portuguese professionals:
   - Each professional may have to **self-assess 23% Portuguese VAT** and file a VAT return for that month (art. 2.º n.º 1 i) CIVA; see A2).
   - PT WSB would have Indonesian income from Portuguese payers, which raises the same effective-management questions.
   
   It is more paperwork on both sides for small amounts.
8. **Alternative:** deduct the referral cut from the client payment. Avoid this. Each professional should invoice the client the full amount and pay the fee separately, so that every recibo matches the money received.

**Written network agreement: SHOULD DO SOON** (before the first shared client). It is not a legal requirement, but it protects everyone. Contents:
- Each professional is an **independent self-employed person**: own clients, own invoices, own liability and professional insurance. There is **no partnership** and no joint invoicing, which avoids being treated as a *sociedade de facto*.
- The fee amount and its basis (monthly flat fee plus referral % only on clients sourced through LUSONUSA), the invoicing date, and payment within 15 days.
- Use of the LUSONUSA brand, website and profiles. Brand ownership stays with Aditya, or with PT WSB if it owns the IP [decide].
- **GDPR:** client data shared through the website. Each professional is a separate controller; Aditya acts as processor for the booking/contact system. A short data-processing clause is enough.
- Non-solicitation and exit terms, quality standards for certified translation, and conflict handling.
- Governing law Portugal; language PT/EN.
- Effort: about 2–3 pages; free if written internally, about €200–500 if reviewed by a lawyer [estimate].
